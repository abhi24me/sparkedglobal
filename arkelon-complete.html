Standards & Certifications

What the credentials mean.

The certifications Arkelon product meets — and what each one actually governs.

🇩🇪
German Licence (VDA)
Governs the AdBlue® trademark. Sets the world's strictest purity thresholds for urea solution — particularly on trace metals that damage injectors and catalysts. Tighter than IS 16282 on copper and zinc limits.
🌐
ISO 22241
The international AUS 32 standard. Four parts: product specification, test methods, handling and transport, in-field testing. Required by all major OEMs globally for SCR warranty compliance.
🇮🇳
IS 16282 (BIS)
India's Bureau of Indian Standards equivalent of ISO 22241. Mandatory for DEF sold in India under BS6 norms. Governs concentration, pH, density, and impurity thresholds.
📋
Certificate of Analysis
A batch-specific lab report tying a lot number to actual test results. The only way to verify that the product you received matches the standard it claims to meet. Arkelon makes this available on request, per batch, before purchase.

Product Overview

Arkelon AUS 32

AUS 32 — Aqueous Urea Solution at 32.5% concentration — is the internationally standardised formulation required by all SCR systems in BS6 diesel vehicles. The concentration is not approximate. ISO 22241 specifies 32.5% ± 0.7% precisely because the SCR reduction reaction is concentration-sensitive: below 31.8%, NOx conversion is incomplete; above 33.2%, excess ammonia triggers catalyst degradation.

Arkelon AUS 32 is produced under a German licence, applying VDA-grade trace metal thresholds that go beyond IS 16282 minimum requirements. The product is manufactured at the same facility supplying India's leading automotive OEMs — meaning the production process, equipment, and QC protocols are maintained at OEM qualification standard.

How It Works

DEF and your SCR system.

Understanding this process explains why concentration precision and purity are not optional — and why the quality of your DEF supplier has a direct impact on SCR system longevity.

🔥
Combustion
Diesel engine produces exhaust gases containing NOx
💧
DEF Injection
AUS 32 injected into the exhaust stream upstream of the SCR catalyst
♨️
Decomposition
Heat converts urea into ammonia (NH₃) — requires precise concentration
⚗️
SCR Reaction
NH₃ reacts with NOx inside the catalyst — sensitive to trace metal contamination
🌿
Clean Exhaust
NOx converted to harmless N₂ and water vapour (H₂O)
Why concentration tolerance matters Below 31.8% urea: incomplete NOx reduction, sensor faults. Above 33.2%: excess ammonia, catalyst degradation, ECU warnings. The ±0.7% tolerance in ISO 22241 is tight. Maintaining it consistently requires manufacturing precision that commodity traders do not achieve.
Storage & Handling

Protecting product quality.

DEF is stable and safe under correct conditions. Poor storage degrades quality before it reaches the vehicle.

🌡️
Temperature
Store between −5°C and +25°C. Freezes at −11°C — thaws back to specification. Degrades above 30°C with prolonged exposure. Keep out of direct sunlight.
🔒
Keep Sealed
DEF absorbs CO₂ from air, raising acidity over time. Keep containers sealed when not dispensing. Reseal completely after use.
🚫
No Metal Containers
Never store in iron, copper, or zinc-alloy containers. DEF leaches trace metals from these surfaces and becomes non-compliant. Use HDPE, stainless steel, or approved plastics only.
📦
Dedicated Equipment
Use dedicated dispensing equipment. Even trace contamination from diesel or coolant will degrade the product. Never share a pump across fluids.

The Regulatory Backdrop

Demand that is structural.

DEF demand in India is driven by regulation, not trend. BS6 emission norms — effective April 2020 — made SCR systems mandatory on all new diesel commercial vehicles. Every BS6 truck, bus, and heavy equipment unit sold since then requires DEF as a fixed operational consumable.

Running an SCR-equipped vehicle without DEF triggers an ECU-enforced engine derate — the vehicle loses power and speed until the tank is replenished. In commercial operations, that means delivery failures and revenue loss. DEF procurement is not discretionary.

India's commercial vehicle fleet is large and growing. The transition from BS4 to BS6 continues to add SCR-equipped vehicles to the installed base each year. DEF consumption compounds as the fleet grows.

2016
Government announces direct leap from BS4 to BS6 — skipping BS5. April 2020 deadline set.
Apr 2020
BS6 Phase I mandatory. SCR systems and DEF requirement standard on all new diesel commercial vehicles.
2023
BS6 Phase II (OBD2). On-board diagnostics enforce DEF quality and presence more rigorously — increasing the cost of using off-spec product.
Ongoing
BS6 fleet grows as BS4 vehicles are retired. DEF demand increases year on year as installed SCR fleet expands.
Future
Potential BS7 alignment will maintain SCR + DEF as the core NOx control technology for diesel.
